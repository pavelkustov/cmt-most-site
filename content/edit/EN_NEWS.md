# News in English

Рабочий файл английских новостей. На сайт переносится командой `python tools/texts/english.py --import`
(вместе с направлениями и людьми), на сайт попадет при следующей сборке (`python tools/build.py`).

Перевод сделан 24.09.2026 по анкете новостей (`content/news.json`), вычитывает владелец. Имена людей
как в `content/edit/EN_PEOPLE.md`, кавычки «елочками», внутренние „лапками“, как в русской версии.
Заголовок разбит на три строки переносами `<br>`, как у русских: строки ровные по длине и влезают
в карточку на компьютере (подобрано замером 24.09.2026). Меняя заголовок, переставьте переносы.
У каждой новости: заголовок (строка `##`), анонс для карточки (`Card`) и разделы текста окна
`Lead` (вступление), `Quote` (цитата), `Note` (заключение), абзацы через пустую строку.
В интервью вопрос узнается по знаку «?» в конце абзаца, первый абзац это вводка.
Новая новость из анкеты появляется здесь пустой заготовкой (`--news`), пока ее нет, на английской
странице она показывается по-русски.

## Scientists of the center <br> and PRMU develop a new <br> approach to treating the liver

<!-- id: liver-mirna -->

**Card:** Scientists from PRMU and ITC «Bridge» created nanoparticles with microRNA-200a for acute liver diseases. In rats, they prevented organ damage and reduced collagen in the tissues.

### Lead

About 2 million people die from liver diseases every year. Chronic diseases are often linked to fibrosis, the overgrowth of connective tissue that leads to cirrhosis and cancer. Acute damage results from poisoning and drug overdose. Existing treatments only relieve the symptoms without restoring the tissue. MicroRNAs that control genes are promising, but they are unstable and need carriers. Scientists from the Privolzhsky Research Medical University (PRMU) and ITC «Bridge» proposed nanoparticles made of biocompatible polylactide to deliver microRNA-200a, which becomes deficient in liver diseases. The approach targets both acute and chronic pathologies while stimulating the regeneration of the organ.

### Quote

«Our part of the work at ITMO was to develop carriers for therapeutic RNA aimed at treating liver fibrosis and acute liver failure in rats. We synthesized polylactic acid nanoparticles and loaded them with therapeutic microRNA, and then our colleagues administered the finished complexes to laboratory animals with induced pathologies. Loading therapeutic RNA into carriers is a complex and highly responsible step. It is not enough simply to deliver the molecule into a cell: you need to keep it stable and active and make sure it ends up strictly in the cytosol. That is why we paid special attention to choosing the right loading strategy that does not damage the RNA», said Mikhail Zyuzin, leading researcher.

### Note

The effectiveness of the approach was tested on rat models of acute liver injury and progressive fibrosis. Targeted delivery of microRNA-200a with polylactide nanoparticles stopped cell death after the first injection, and in chronic disease it reversed fibrosis by two stages. The study also involved researchers from Sechenov First Moscow State Medical University, the Volga District Medical Center of the Federal Medical and Biological Agency and the Nizhny Novgorod Regional Clinical Oncology Dispensary.

## Our new paper <br> in Nature Communications <br> on unclonable labels

<!-- id: nature-comms-2025 -->

**Card:** Our paper has been published in Nature Communications: multiresonant gold–silicon particles made it possible to create physically unclonable functions with a record information density.

### Lead

Physically unclonable functions (PUFs) are systems with random structural features that arise uncontrollably during manufacturing. With proper optical readout and computer processing, they can serve as sources of unique keys for anti-counterfeiting or cryptography. A logical next step for such systems is to shrink the characteristic scale of their randomness to the nanoscale, which would make possible higher-capacity sources of cryptographic keys. Such a transition, however, faces fundamental problems related to the diffraction limit of optical readout.

### Note

Members of our center, together with colleagues from Université de Lorraine and New Uzbekistan University, proposed a solution to this problem using multiresonant gold–silicon particles. The unique spectral features of these particles’ broadband photoluminescence were used as «fingerprints» of their nanoscale structural inhomogeneities. As a result, an ultrahigh storage density of unique information in these physically unclonable functions was achieved, at least 2–3 orders of magnitude higher than values reported in other works. The paper is published in Nature Communications (IF = 14.9): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60121-9

### Note

Nanomaterials have been used by humankind for a long time, long before the term «nanomaterials» became popular. ## Our scientists create <br> silicon-based unclonable <br> security labels

<!-- id: unclonable-labels-2023 -->

**Card:** Our team has developed unclonable labels to protect goods from counterfeiting, based on laser-formed silicon nanoparticles.

### Lead

Members of our team have learned to create unclonable labels that protect goods from counterfeiting. These are images made of silicon nanoparticles formed with a laser. The color, structure and arrangement of the silicon particles cannot be controlled: these parameters are distributed randomly, which makes the label impossible to forge. To check a label for authenticity, it is enough to upload its photo to a special program that gives a verdict instantly. The results were published in ACS Applied Nano Materials.

### Quote

«Imagine you have a handful of sand and decide to pour it onto a plate. As soon as the last grain falls, you will see a pattern. You will not be able to repeat the same pattern on another plate. The unclonability of our security technology is based on this principle. Instead of sand we took silicon: the laser acts on a silicon film, and nanoparticles are „knocked out“ of it and randomly form a texture. The final picture looks like a cluster of multicolored stars», says the first author of the paper, PhD student Pavel Kustov.

### Note

The technology, developed in the research group of Dmitry Zuev, senior researcher at ITMO’s New Physics and Technology Institute, is based on laser ablation, namely the transfer of a silicon film onto glass by a laser pulse. The overall shape of the label can be set in advance, for example in the shape of a brand logo. The parameters of the silicon nanoparticles that form the picture, however, cannot be programmed. Every image is unique and impossible to repeat. Silicon was chosen for a reason: compared with other substances, it is affordable, resistant to external influences and offers a wide range of colors. It can also be applied to a wider range of materials. Each silicon particle is assigned unique data: coordinates of its position and color, as well as its degree of crystallinity (structure). All these parameters are encoded and entered into a database. The position coordinates define the shape of the label and provide the first level of protection. The chromatic coordinates are responsible for the color palette and raise reliability by another step. Finally, the degree of crystallinity, the last level of protection, reflects how ordered or chaotic the atoms in each particle are. «Our labels are invisible to the naked eye. To see them and check their authenticity, you need to take a photo of the label with a magnifying lens or a microscope and upload it to a specially written program. Using machine learning and computer vision, the program aligns and clusters the photo, that is, simplifies it to a kind of paint-by-numbers picture where each number corresponds to a certain color, and then compares it with the database. If all parameters match, the program will conclude that you have the original. If there are too many differences, it will point to a fake», explains co-author Elena Petrova, a student at ITMO’s New Physics and Technology Institute. According to the authors, the labels are ideal for makers of luxury goods or products released in limited quantities: medicines, cars, jewelry, musical instruments. «There are very few studies in the international scientific community that offer affordable and easy-to-implement technologies for creating unclonable security labels. Laser ablation and silicon are used for this purpose for the first time. It turned out that a relatively simple method can provide the highest level of protection. The next step in the project is to make our labels more flexible to expand the range of materials they can be applied to», concluded co-author Martin Sandomirskii, a student.
